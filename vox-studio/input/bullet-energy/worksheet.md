# Where Does the Bullet's Energy Go? Companion worksheet

Cambridge IGCSE Physics (0625): Energy, work and power (1.7) and Momentum (1.6, Extended). Use it alongside the video `final-video.mp4`.

---

## 1. Energy flow (Sankey-style)

```
CHEMICAL STORE (gunpowder) ════════════════════════╗
                                                   ║  ──► thermal + sound in the gun   (wasted)
                                                   ▼
                  KINETIC STORE of the bullet ═════════════╗
                                                           ║ ──► air resistance → thermal store of the air
                                                           ▼
                          WORK DONE by the wood's resistive force
                             ├──► thermal store of bullet + wood   (largest share)
                             ├──► deforming / splitting the wood
                             └──► sound
```

*The arrow widths are illustrative. The total energy out always equals the total energy in.*

**Efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input (× 100%)**

## 2. Worked examples (try each step before you look)

**Example 1.** Find the kinetic energy of a 10 g bullet moving at 400 m/s.

1. Convert the mass: m = 10 g = 10 ÷ 1000 = **0.010 kg**
2. E_k = ½ m v²
3. E_k = ½ × 0.010 kg × (400 m/s)² = ½ × 0.010 × 160 000
4. **E_k = 800 J**

**Example 2.** The bullet stops in 8 cm of wood. Find the average resistive force.

1. All 800 J is removed by work done on the bullet, so W = 800 J.
2. Convert the distance: d = 8 cm = **0.08 m**
3. W = F d, so F = W ÷ d = 800 J ÷ 0.08 m
4. **F = 10 000 N** (1.0 × 10⁴ N)

**Example 3 (Extended).** The 10 g bullet at 400 m/s embeds in a 2.0 kg block on a smooth surface.

1. Momentum before = m₁u₁ = 0.010 kg × 400 m/s = 4.0 kg m/s
2. Momentum after = (m₁ + m₂) v = 2.010 kg × v
3. Momentum is conserved: 4.0 = 2.010 v, so **v = 1.99 m/s** (2.0 m/s to 2 s.f.)
4. E_k before = 800 J
5. E_k after = ½ × 2.010 kg × (1.99 m/s)² ≈ **4.0 J**
6. Energy transferred away from the kinetic store ≈ 800 − 4 = **796 J** (over 99%)
7. **Where did it go?** To the thermal store of the bullet and block, to sound, and to deforming the wood. The kinetic energy is not conserved, so this is an **inelastic collision**. Total energy is still conserved.

## 3. Exam-style questions (Paper 4 style)

**Q1 (Describe) [3]** A bullet is fired from a gun into a wooden block, where it stops. Describe the energy transfers that take place from firing to stopping.

**Q2 (Calculate) [5]** A bullet of mass 15 g travels at 350 m/s. It stops after penetrating 0.12 m into a block of wood.
(a) Calculate the kinetic energy of the bullet. [3]
(b) Calculate the average resistive force exerted by the wood on the bullet. [2]

**Q3 (Explain) [3]** A bullet embeds itself in a block resting on a smooth surface, and they move off together. Explain why momentum is conserved in this collision but kinetic energy is not.

## 4. Mark scheme (how an examiner marks it)

**Q1 [3]: any three of the following, one mark each**

- B1: energy is transferred from the **chemical store** (of the propellant/gunpowder) to the **kinetic store** of the bullet
- B1: some energy is transferred to the **thermal store** of the gun/surroundings, and as **sound**, when it is fired
- B1: during flight, energy is transferred to the **thermal store of the air** by **air resistance** (work done against air resistance)
- B1: in the block, **work is done** by the resistive force, and the bullet's kinetic store goes to the **thermal store** of the bullet and wood, to **sound**, and to **deforming** the wood
- *Where marks are lost:* "the energy is used up" or "destroyed" scores 0 for that point. Naming a store without saying where the energy goes ("there is heat") is usually not enough. Be specific: "transferred to the thermal store of the wood".

**Q2(a) [3]**

- C1: m = 15 g = **0.015 kg** (conversion seen)
- C1: E_k = ½ m v² = ½ × 0.015 × 350²
- A1: **E_k = 920 J** (918.75 J; accept 919 J or 920 J)
- *Where marks are lost:* using m = 15 gives 918 750 J, which earns the formula mark only (max 1). Forgetting to square v gives 2.6 J, which earns the conversion mark only.

**Q2(b) [2]**

- C1: W = F d, so F = W ÷ d = 918.75 ÷ 0.12
- A1: **F = 7700 N** (7656 N; accept 7600–7700 N; error carried forward from (a) is allowed)
- *Where marks are lost:* missing or wrong units (for example "7656 J") lose the A1.

**Q3 [3]**

- B1: momentum is conserved because there are **no external (resultant) forces** on the bullet and block system (the surface is smooth); the forces between the bullet and the block are equal and opposite
- B1: kinetic energy is **not conserved** because some is transferred to the **thermal store** of the block and bullet, to **sound**, and to **deforming** the block
- B1: so the collision is **inelastic** (but **total energy is still conserved**)
- *Where marks are lost:* saying "energy is lost/destroyed" without saying where it goes, or claiming momentum is lost too.

## 5. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| "The energy is destroyed / used up" | Energy is **conserved**. It is **dissipated** to the surroundings, mostly as thermal energy. |
| E_k = ½ m v (forgetting to square) | E_k = ½ m **v²**. Square the speed first: 400² = 160 000. |
| Using m = 10 instead of 0.010 | Convert **g → kg** (÷ 1000) and **cm → m** (÷ 100) before substituting. |
| Assuming E_k before = E_k after when the bullet embeds | **Momentum** is conserved; **kinetic energy is not**. That is what makes the collision inelastic. |
| Writing answers with no units | Energy in **J**, force in **N**, speed in **m/s**, mass in **kg**, distance in **m**, momentum in **kg m/s**. |

## 6. Summary for your notes

- **Energy transfers:** the chemical store (gunpowder) goes to the bullet's kinetic store. In the target, work done by the resistive force transfers it to the thermal stores of the bullet and wood, to sound, and to deforming the wood.
- **Kinetic energy:** E_k = ½ m v². Convert g → kg first and square the speed. A 10 g bullet at 400 m/s has 800 J.
- **Work done = force × distance:** W = F d. Stopping 800 J in 0.08 m needs an average force of F = 800 ÷ 0.08 = 10 000 N.
- **Energy is always conserved:** the "lost" energy is **dissipated** to the surroundings (mainly as thermal energy and sound) and can't easily be used again. Efficiency = useful output ÷ total input.
- **Extended:** in an **inelastic collision** momentum is conserved (m₁u₁ = (m₁ + m₂)v gives v ≈ 1.99 m/s), but kinetic energy is not. Here it drops from 800 J to about 4 J, so about 796 J goes to thermal energy, sound and deformation.
