# Fact-check sources — The Cold Chain

- **Most refrigerated vaccines are stored between 2°C and 8°C (36°F–46°F).**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
- **Heat exposure reduces vaccine potency; exposure to freezing temperatures can permanently damage many refrigerated vaccines, and loss of potency cannot be seen.**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit; WHO, Immunization in Practice (Module 3: The cold chain) — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
- **The cold chain spans manufacturer, distribution and the provider, using refrigerated transport and qualified insulated containers with conditioned cold packs.**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit (Transport section); WHO Immunization in Practice — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
- **Digital data loggers continuously record storage temperatures.**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit (Temperature Monitoring Equipment) — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
- **Vaccine vial monitors are heat-sensitive labels; if the inner square matches or is darker than the outer circle, the vaccine should not be used.**
  WHO, Vaccine Vial Monitor guidance — https://www.who.int/teams/immunization-vaccines-and-biologicals/essential-programme-on-immunization/supply-chain
- **Use purpose-built/dedicated vaccine storage units and check and record temperatures at the start of each workday.**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
- **After a temperature excursion, label vaccines 'Do Not Use', keep them stored at proper temperature, and contact the manufacturer or immunization program before using or discarding.**
  CDC, Vaccine Storage and Handling Toolkit (Temperature Excursions) — https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/storage-handling/
