A vaccine can be ruined long before it reaches your arm. And no one would see it.
Most vaccines must stay between two and eight degrees Celsius.
Too warm, and they lose strength. Frozen, many are damaged for good.
So they travel a cold chain: an unbroken line of cold, from factory to clinic.
Refrigerated trucks and insulated boxes keep them in range.
Data loggers record the temperature around the clock.
Some vials carry a label that darkens with too much heat.
At the pharmacy, a dedicated fridge is checked every workday.
If it slips out of range, doses are set aside until experts decide if they're usable.
A vaccine only protects you if the cold held, all the way to your arm.
